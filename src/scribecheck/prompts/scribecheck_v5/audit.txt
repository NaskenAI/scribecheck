For every candidate id, return exactly one entry:
- "keep": false if the transcript does not establish it (or only a weaker or hedged version), if

- "core": true if a correct note of this consultation must carry it; false if it is contextual

  refer; safety-net instructions; numeric thresholds. Attendance and appointment logistics are

  person, how or when an appointment is booked or a call will come, and transport or travel
  arrangements.
- "severity": grade the consequence if the note OMITTED this fact, using this rubric
  (Fox et al., OmissionBench harness, MIT):

   "critical" - the omission would plausibly change clinical action, delay or misdirect care, or
     create a safety risk. Anchors: an omitted working diagnosis, or a hedged impression that
     would harden into certainty; omitted red-flag safety-netting; an omitted or wrong drug,
     allergy, dose, frequency, duration or interaction; a conditional plan made definite; an
     omitted examination that did happen; an omitted pertinent negative that licenses the
     management decision; patient identity attributes used clinically.
   "supporting" - degrades the note's completeness, clarity or defensibility, but is unlikely to
     change what happens next. Anchors: duration or onset detail that colours but does not gate
     the plan; social context relevant to follow-up; nuance lost without meaning being inverted;
     a secondary symptom that neither supports nor threatens the diagnosis.
   "peripheral" - no plausible clinical consequence. Anchors: conversational colour, rapport,
     patient-education phrasing, administrative closure, redundant restatement.

   Decide in order: (1) would a competent clinician plausibly DO something different if this
   fact were missing? yes -> critical. (2) does its absence remove a safety net or create a
   latent risk? yes -> critical. (3) does its absence materially weaken the note as a record?
   yes -> supporting. (4) otherwise peripheral. If torn between two grades, take the lower one.
